H1: Planning a Misting Playground: Shade, Water and Site Decisions

Every H2 begins with its answer. Proposed sections:

1. What should a misting playground brief actually specify? — define play experience, optional mist, shade, operations and responsibilities; distinguish splash-pad selection. Evidence: strategy and editorial procurement framework.
2. Will mist improve comfort in this climate? — conditional evaporation, humidity/wind, validation and heat procedures. Evidence: PNNL evaporative principles, with building-equipment scope caveat; CPSC heat guidance.
3. How should shade and mist work together? — different mechanisms, sun timing and serviceable layout. Evidence: CPSC §2.1.1; G4 for structural questions.
4. How can wet and dry activities share the site? — map drift, wet surfaces, exits, routes and dry-mode operation; no dimensioned engineering advice. Evidence: CPSC layout and surfacing guidance; CDC water controls; editorial interface checklist.
5. What water and maintenance arrangements must be agreed? — source, storage, stagnation, access and accountable operator. Evidence: CDC other-devices module and aquatic toolkit where applicable.
6. How should buyers compare scope and whole-life cost? — itemized scope/responsibility table, no unsupported price benchmarks. Editorial recommendation.
7. What information should you send before requesting a proposal? — country, hours, weather, age/users, survey, utilities, operator and budget; CTA to commercial inquiry with scope limitations.
8. What else do buyers ask before committing? — nine distinct procurement questions with cited answers: retrofit; area; water source; water/power calculation; rain vs shade; playground vs pool slide; certification; handover; seasonal shutdown. These are editorial questions informed by public sources, not invented customer interviews or PAA.
9. Which sources inform this guide? — dated primary source list with jurisdiction and limits; transparent AI-assisted preparation and no invented human qualifications.
